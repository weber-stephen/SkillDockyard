# Skill Dockyard brand strategy

## Strategic summary

Skill Dockyard is the shared workspace where teams review, publish, install, and maintain reusable skills for AI tools.

The brand should help a prospective customer understand that Skill Dockyard is not merely a directory or storage system. It provides a controlled path from a useful private instruction to a trusted, published skill that teammates can install and keep current.

The brand should feel:

- Operational rather than experimental
- Trustworthy rather than restrictive
- Technical without excluding capable workplace users
- Deliberate rather than bureaucratic
- Useful rather than futuristic

## Positioning

### Target customer

Skill Dockyard is primarily designed for:

- Engineering and platform leaders standardizing how teams use AI tools
- AI enablement teams responsible for reusable workflows
- Reviewers responsible for quality, compatibility, and safety
- Workplace teams that create and reuse skills across tools such as Codex and Claude Code

The buyer is likely to be an engineering, platform, security, operations, or AI enablement leader. The end user may be any capable workplace user who creates, improves, installs, or uses a skill.

### Problem

Useful AI instructions become scattered across chats, documents, local folders, and repositories. Teams cannot easily tell:

- Which version should be used
- Who created or manages a skill
- Whether a change has been reviewed
- Whether a skill uses tools or connectors that require attention
- Whether an installed copy is current or has local changes

### Existing alternatives

Customers currently rely on:

- Chat histories and copied prompts
- Documents and internal wikis
- Local skill folders
- Source-code repositories
- Public skill directories
- Marketplaces tied to a single AI tool
- Informal review through messages or meetings
- Doing nothing and allowing each person to maintain their own version

### Unique capability

Skill Dockyard combines the skill lifecycle in one workspace:

- Import installed skills
- Keep private drafts private
- Submit new skills and updates for review
- Compare submitted and published versions
- Publish a trusted version
- Share skills without transferring ownership
- Download and install skills across supported AI tools
- Check for and install published updates
- Preserve permissions, decisions, and review history

### Customer outcome

Teams gain one dependable place to find the current, reviewed version of a reusable AI workflow. Improvements can spread without allowing unreviewed changes to silently replace what the team relies on.

### Positioning statement

> For teams that need to standardize reusable AI workflows, Skill Dockyard is a shared skill management platform that helps people review, publish, install, and maintain trusted skills. Unlike scattered documents, repositories, and vendor-specific directories, Skill Dockyard keeps ownership, changes, access, and installed versions clear across AI tools.

### Category description

Use **shared skill management platform** when a category label is needed.

Use **shared workspace for trusted AI skills** when a more approachable description is appropriate.

Do not lead with **registry**, **repository**, **marketplace**, or **governance platform** in general product copy. Those terms either understate the workflow, suggest a different business model, or introduce infrastructure language before the customer understands the benefit.

## Name strategy

### Evaluation

**Skill Dockyard scores 7.4 out of 10.** It is a good name with one meaningful naming risk.

The name should be retained for the current product, subject to professional trademark clearance.

### Why the name works

**Skill** provides the category anchor and matches the primary object in the product. It supports clear interface language such as **Add a skill**, **Share skill**, and **Install update**.

**Dockyard** expresses more than storage. A dockyard receives, inspects, repairs, maintains, and prepares things for use. That maps well to importing, reviewing, publishing, installing, and updating skills.

The complete name is more memorable and distinctive than a generic category name such as “Skill Registry.”

### Naming risks

- **Skill** can be mistaken for employee training or talent management when it appears without an AI context.
- **Dockyard** does not explain the software by itself.
- DockYard is already used by an established software and AI development consultancy, and Dockyard is used by other technology products.
- Customers may shorten the name to Dockyard, increasing confusion and weakening search ownership.
- The name may become limiting if the product expands from skills into complete agent-runtime management.

### Naming rules

- Use the complete name **Skill Dockyard** in public-facing contexts.
- Do not shorten the product name to **Dockyard**.
- Pair the name with an explicit description until the category is well established.
- Use the maritime idea as a restrained strategic metaphor, not as themed interface language.
- Do not use nautical names for product actions, navigation, roles, or statuses.

Recommended name lockup descriptor:

> Skill Dockyard — shared AI skills, reviewed and ready to use.

### Domain strategy

At the time of the initial brand review on September 20, 2026, registry queries returned no registration record for:

- `skilldockyard.com`
- `skill-dockyard.com`
- `skilldockyard.ai`
- `skilldockyard.dev`

Domain status can change at any time and must be reconfirmed before purchase. Use `skilldockyard.com` as the primary domain if available. Redirect the other domains to it.

Domain availability is not trademark clearance. Complete a professional trademark search before making a significant identity or launch investment.

## Messaging architecture

### One-sentence description

Skill Dockyard gives teams one place to review, publish, share, install, and update reusable skills for AI tools.

### Core value proposition

Turn scattered AI instructions into trusted, reusable skills that stay current across the team.

### Messaging pillars

#### 1. One trusted version

**Customer problem:** People copy and modify instructions until nobody knows which version to use.

**Product capability:** Skill Dockyard separates private drafts, submitted versions, and the published version.

**Customer benefit:** Teammates can confidently start from the current version without preventing continued improvement.

**Evidence:** Version comparison, explicit publishing decisions, status tracking, and review history.

**Example messaging:**

> Give everyone a dependable version to build from.

#### 2. Improvements that survive

**Customer problem:** Useful improvements remain trapped in a chat, document, or personal copy.

**Product capability:** People can add skills or submit updates through a structured review process.

**Customer benefit:** Better ways of working become reusable team knowledge.

**Evidence:** New-skill submissions, update submissions, change summaries, and notifications.

**Example messaging:**

> When someone finds a better way, make it available to everyone.

#### 3. Review before release

**Customer problem:** An unreviewed change can silently alter a workflow that others depend on.

**Product capability:** Reviewers compare submitted and published versions, inspect review notes, and explicitly publish, request changes, or reject.

**Customer benefit:** Teams can improve skills without giving up control over what becomes available.

**Evidence:** Server-enforced permissions, version comparison, review decisions, and compatibility and safety checks.

**Example messaging:**

> See exactly what changed before the team starts using it.

#### 4. Clear ownership and access

**Customer problem:** Sharing often obscures who owns something and who is allowed to change it.

**Product capability:** Skill Dockyard keeps creation, workspace management, maintenance contacts, sharing permissions, and review authority distinct.

**Customer benefit:** Collaboration can expand without silently transferring ownership or publishing authority.

**Evidence:** Workspace roles, skill shares, invitations, revocation, and preserved review history.

**Example messaging:**

> Share useful skills without losing track of ownership or control.

#### 5. Updates that reach the tools people use

**Customer problem:** A published improvement has little value if installed copies remain outdated.

**Product capability:** Connected computers can import skills, check for updates, install published versions, and detect local changes.

**Customer benefit:** Teams can keep skills current across supported AI tools without overwriting local work unexpectedly.

**Evidence:** Import, download, installation reporting, update checks, and local-change protection.

**Example messaging:**

> Publish once. Help every teammate stay current.

## Brand personality

### We are operational

**This means:** We focus on real workflows, states, responsibilities, and next actions.

**We are not:** Cold, mechanical, or obsessed with infrastructure.

### We are deliberate

**This means:** Changes move through understandable review and publishing steps.

**We are not:** Bureaucratic, slow, or designed to stop people from contributing.

### We are trustworthy

**This means:** We explain ownership, access, version status, and review notes precisely.

**We are not:** Alarmist or willing to imply that a check guarantees safety.

### We are capable

**This means:** We respect technical users and reveal meaningful detail when it helps them make a decision.

**We are not:** Filled with unexplained jargon or complexity for its own sake.

### We are practical

**This means:** We describe what the product does and the outcome it creates.

**We are not:** Futuristic, grandiose, or dependent on vague AI claims.

## Voice principles

### Website

Lead with the customer outcome, then explain the mechanism. Qualify **skills** with **AI skills**, **AI instructions**, or **AI workflows** early enough to avoid confusion with employee training.

Prefer:

> Keep your team’s AI skills reviewed and current.

Avoid:

> Revolutionize your organization with a cutting-edge AI skill ecosystem.

### Product interface

Use the terminology defined in `AGENTS.md`. State what happened, what it affects, and what the user can do next.

Prefer:

> This update is awaiting review. The published version remains available to teammates.

Avoid:

> Proposal pending approval.

### Documentation

Use precise technical terms when needed, but introduce them with plain-language context. Prefer concrete examples and commands over abstract platform claims.

### Sales

Connect the product to version consistency, review responsibility, cross-tool portability, and the cost of duplicated work. Do not sell fear or claim that the product eliminates all skill-related risk.

### Social media

Teach the emerging operational discipline around reusable AI instructions. Show specific problems, product workflows, and lessons rather than posting generic commentary about the future of AI.

### Thought leadership

Own the idea that reusable AI skills require the same clarity teams expect from other shared operational knowledge: an accountable owner, an inspectable change, a published version, and a safe way to distribute updates.

## Visual identity direction

### Logo

Use a compact wordmark with an optional symbol that communicates controlled arrival, inspection, version movement, or readiness.

The current anchor is serviceable but literal. If the identity is revisited, explore a symbol based on:

- Two changes converging into one published version
- A protected arrival point
- Version tracks or review gates
- A structured workspace or berth

Avoid:

- Ships, sails, waves, ropes, steering wheels, and lighthouses
- Generic AI sparkles
- Robot heads or brains
- Meaningless geometric technology marks
- Container imagery that makes the product look like a deployment platform

### Typography

Use a compact, highly legible grotesk or neo-grotesk sans serif for interface and body text. A restrained display face may add editorial authority to marketing headings, but it should not reduce clarity.

Typography should feel precise and operational rather than playful, nautical, or futuristic.

### Color

Use the Operational Cobalt system defined in [brand-system.md](brand-system.md). Cobalt is the primary action and structural brand color, chosen to communicate precision, trust, and operational maturity to engineering, platform, security, and AI enablement teams. Distinctiveness should come from disciplined composition, compact typography, cool neutral surfaces, and the restrained tangerine review accent rather than novelty effects.

Support it with:

- Cool off-white and ice-blue backgrounds
- Graphite-blue text
- Restrained blue-gray surfaces and borders
- Tangerine for items needing review or attention
- Red only for destructive actions or confirmed errors

Do not use gradients as a substitute for hierarchy. Filled cobalt surfaces must use the documented light foreground token. Do not introduce green as a primary brand or action color.

### Imagery

Prioritize:

1. Real product screenshots
2. Version comparisons
3. Simple workflow diagrams
4. Skill status and installation views
5. Concrete examples of a skill moving from private draft to published use

Use abstract graphics sparingly. Avoid stock photography, decorative 3D objects, and maritime scenes.

### Relationship to the product interface

The marketing site may express more personality through scale, typography, and composition. The product interface should remain compact, quiet, and task-focused.

Carry the brand into the application through:

- Typography
- Color tokens
- Direct language
- Strong status hierarchy
- Consistent borders and spacing
- Clear review and publishing states

Do not make the application decorative merely to reinforce the brand metaphor.

## Competitive territory

Many competitors describe the category using **registry**, **catalog**, **marketplace**, **governance**, and **security**. These terms provide clarity but are difficult to own and often make products sound interchangeable.

Skill Dockyard should own this territory:

> **The operational home where a team’s AI skills become trusted, current, and reusable.**

Follow category conventions where they build confidence:

- Explain that skills are reusable instructions for AI tools
- Demonstrate versioning, permissions, and review
- Show supported tools
- Make safety and compatibility information visible
- Provide technical documentation for installation and administration

Deliberately break category conventions where they create sameness:

- Lead with the customer workflow rather than “AI governance”
- Present review as enabling improvement rather than blocking it
- Use a grounded editorial identity rather than purple gradients and AI imagery
- Describe concrete product behavior instead of making broad security claims

## Brand guardrails

### Keep

- The complete Skill Dockyard name
- The operational and trustworthy personality
- The review-to-publish workflow as the central story
- Plain-language terminology from `AGENTS.md`
- Operational Cobalt, neutral surfaces, restrained attention color, and product-led evidence

### Improve

- Qualify **skills** with an AI context in first-touch marketing
- Explain the category immediately below or beside the name
- Emphasize install and update capabilities in addition to review
- Add proof through screenshots, supported-tool evidence, and customer outcomes
- Ensure all marketing language uses **publish** rather than **approve** for the user-visible decision

### Change

- Any tendency to shorten the brand to **Dockyard**
- Maritime-themed calls to action or product vocabulary
- Claims that compatibility checks or review notes guarantee safety
- Generic descriptions such as “transform your workflow” or “the future of AI work”

### Missing

- Professional trademark clearance
- Customer evidence and quantified outcomes
- A short category descriptor used consistently with the wordmark
- Documented rules for partner, integration, and supported-tool logo use
- A proof-led product demonstration showing the complete lifecycle

## Evaluation and decision rule

Retain the Skill Dockyard name while all of the following remain true:

- Skills remain the primary product object.
- The core product value remains review, publishing, sharing, installation, and maintenance.
- The complete name can be used consistently.
- Trademark clearance does not reveal an unacceptable conflict.
- Customer research shows that the dockyard metaphor feels operational rather than confusing.

Revisit the name if the product expands primarily into runtime agent management, if customers consistently mistake it for employee training software, or if legal and search conflicts make the complete name difficult to own.
