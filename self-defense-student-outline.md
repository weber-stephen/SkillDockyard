# Self-Defense Student Outline

> Transcription of a student handout. Its legal statements are jurisdiction-dependent and have not been independently verified.

- Intro
- General Notes
- Non-deadly force
  - Part 1, reasonably believed of imminent danger against body
  - Part 2, reasonably believed immediate use of force was necessary to defend against danger
  - Part 3, used no more force than was reasonable necessary to defend against danger
- Deadly Force
  - Part 1, reasonably believed of imminent danger of being killed or great bodily injury
  - Part 2, reasonably believed immediate use of deadly force was necessary against the danger
  - Part 3, used no more force than reasonably necessary to defend against the danger
- Defending a place you are sleeping at, a sleep over (not your home)
  - Part 1, reasonably believed deadly force is
  - Part 2, necessary to prevent entry into the dwelling by a person
  - Part 3, who intends to commit robbery, burglary, arson, rape, kidnapping, child molestation, in the dwelling.
- Defending the place you actually live at (defending your home)
  - Part 1, presumed to have a reasonable fear of imminent death or serious bodily injury,
  - Part 2, if using deadly force against a known intruder who unlawfully and forcibly entered your residence.
  - Part 3, use proportional deadly force, enough force to end the threat.
- Defending Others
  - Part 1, One reasonably believes that the person being assisted had a right to use force in their own defense.
  - Part 2, person being assisted if being confronted with non-deadly force or deadly force, you can only respond with that same force.
- Other Considerations and Potential Liability for exceeding your right to self defense
  - Lawful exercise of self defense, not required to provide medical aide
  - Examples of exceeding your right to self defense
